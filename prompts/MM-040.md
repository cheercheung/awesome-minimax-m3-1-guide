## What's in the workspace
Everything James left you sits under ``Meridian_RiskSnapshot_2026-05-28/``:
  - ``Meridian_RiskSnapshot_2026-05-28/01_Data/positions.csv`` — current position dump, 18 legs across 5
    underlyings. One row per leg, with signed quantities (positive =
    long, negative = short), strike, expiry, entry price, entry date,
    and a free-text strategy tag James used to group related legs.
  - ``Meridian_RiskSnapshot_2026-05-28/01_Data/market_snapshot.csv`` — market snapshot taken at the same time James left
    (16:00 ET close mark). One row per underlying with spot, flat implied vol,
    risk-free rate, and continuous dividend yield.
  - ``Meridian_RiskSnapshot_2026-05-28/01_Data/book_history.csv`` — trade blotter extract; gives you the entry premium
    and trade date for every leg (overlaps with positions.csv but
    sourced from a different system if you want to cross-check).
  - ``Meridian_RiskSnapshot_2026-05-28/02_Inbox/cio_request.md`` — Rachel's email, also above for reference.
  - ``Meridian_RiskSnapshot_2026-05-28/03_Reference/desk_conventions.md`` — the desk's internal pricing conventions
    (model, day count, Greek scaling, contract specs, current limits).
    James maintains this; treat it as the source of truth for how the
    desk wants risk reported.
## What Rachel needs from you
Save exactly these three files directly under `/workspace/deliverables`:
  1. **`risk_engine.py`** — rerunnable code for the pricing and risk math.
  2. **`risk_report.xlsx`** — an auditable workbook with the calculations
     and supporting evidence.
  3. **`risk_summary.md`** — a concise memo to Rachel, dated May 28, 2026 (the supplied EOD snapshot date), that
     leads with material risks, actions, and the supporting rationale.
Use the desk conventions and source data rather than hardcoding final
answers. Organize the workbook and memo in whatever clear, reviewable form
best supports the conclusions. Tie important claims to source rows or
reproducible code, reconcile the memo to the workbook, and explain any
approximation or residual.
Use plain English and enough context for a reviewer to reproduce the
headline conclusions. Do not hide material exceptions in footnotes.

## Public calculation-evidence contract
Workbook conclusions must be supported by formulas or an explicitly linked and reperformable calculation chain that reaches the supplied source rows and assumptions; isolated formula-count tabs, pasted final values, or prose-only calculations are insufficient.
Delivered code must run against the public inputs and regenerate the operative calculations and outputs; hard-coded headline values do not establish support.
For every material reported result, identify the source basis, unit, sign convention and operative selected value, and reconcile that value across all deliverables. If a calculation cannot be made from the supplied evidence, disclose the missing dependency and its decision effect rather than inventing it.
Required final deliverables (use these exact relative filenames):
- risk_engine.py
- risk_report.xlsx
- risk_summary.md
  Save each required file under /workspace/deliverables. Do not translate, rename, or replace these filenames.
